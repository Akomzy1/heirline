# Heirline should earn trust through restraint

The research says Heirline should be built as a calm, readable "document" page with one signature scroll moment, not an immersive WebGL experience. The incumbent private banks (Pictet, C. Hoare & Co, Rothschild & Co, Lombard Odier) share a grammar: a one-sentence hero with a single action, a band of dated facts right after it, heritage told as a dated record, about five navigation items, and a closing invitation to "begin the conversation". Award juries reward heavy motion, but the same scorecards show usability and accessibility falling behind. Cartier's 2025 microsite scored 9.00 for animation and 6.80 for accessibility. A typography-led law and notary site (Kümmerlein, 2025) won recognition without any WebGL, which is the closest model for Heirline's positioning. The 2026 web platform makes restraint cheap. CSS scroll-driven animations reached Baseline in October 2026, cross-document view transitions work in Chrome and Safari, and GSAP is now entirely free. The binding limits come from WCAG and the audience: older principals, mostly on iPhones and variable Nigerian mobile networks. Two caveats govern everything below. First, the reference-site notes could read **page text and markup only, not rendered pages**, so every claim about those sites' motion, typefaces, colour and easing is structural or inferred, not observed. Second, the research on **Nigerian and diaspora HNI trust signals and sourced facts did not complete**. That section is an open gap, and this report contains no figures for it.

## Incumbent private banks lead with one sentence and prove with dated records

The best incumbent sites open with a single claim and a single action. **Pictet's hero is just a question, a follow-up and one link**: "How do you like your calm?" / "Before, during or after the storm?" / "Find out more" ([Pictet](https://www.pictet.com/)). C. Hoare & Co uses a kicker ("Private Banking"), one headline ("Expertise, empathy and exceptional flexibility") and a "SCROLL" cue ([C. Hoare & Co](https://www.hoaresbank.co.uk/)). Rothschild & Co runs a muted ambient loop of a lobby revolving door under a one-line positioning statement ([Rothschild & Co](https://www.rothschildandco.com/en/)). Heirline's brief rules out people, so the analogue is an object-led loop or still (a ledger page, a seal, brass hardware, a survey plan). Lombard Odier is the counter-example: a **five-slide rotating hero carousel** of competing editorial messages, which reads closer to template than to restraint ([Lombard Odier](https://www.lombardodier.com/)).

Navigation is minimal and grown-up. Hoare hides **five items** (Customers, About Us, Philanthropy, News, Contact) behind a "MENU" toggle next to "LOGIN". Its logo comes in standard and white variants, which suggests the header switches between light and dark over hero media ([C. Hoare & Co](https://www.hoaresbank.co.uk/)). Rothschild opens **full-width panels with uppercase group labels** ("WHO WE ARE", "OPPORTUNITIES") and explicit "Back"/"Close menu" controls ([Rothschild & Co](https://www.rothschildandco.com/en/)). Pictet's header carries only a logo, a region selector and "Client login" ([Pictet](https://www.pictet.com/)).

The scroll storytelling is a rhythm of statements and proof, not spectacle. Pictet moves from "Who we are" to a **four-figure proof band with an "as at" date** (assets under custody at 30 Sep 2026, capital ratio, liquidity coverage, headcount). It then runs a "220 years of Pictet" module of eight pivotal moments in archival images, followed by a timeline of Geneva addresses by year ([Pictet](https://www.pictet.com/)). Hoare states "A family business since 1672" across **12 generations**, and leads its insights with "Alexander's Summer Letter 2026", a recurring dated letter from the senior partner ([C. Hoare & Co](https://www.hoaresbank.co.uk/)). Two lessons transfer directly. Heritage is told as a ledger of dated entries, which suits a firm whose product is a register. And the senior-partner letter is a ready-made format for Heirline's calm partner voice, in place of a blog. Heirline cannot copy the AUM band: it is young, it shows no client figures, and its brief bans unsourced statistics. The transferable idea is *precision* rather than size. The proof band should describe the process (a verified register, title checks, a 2-of-3 sealed vault, an independent chartered-accountant sign-off), not boast about scale.

The closing moves are quiet and partly regulatory. Rothschild ends with "Contact us to begin the conversation", a fraud "Clarification" notice and "Back to top" ([Rothschild & Co](https://www.rothschildandco.com/en/)). Lombard Odier's footer states that services "cannot be acquired through the website" and points users to a Relationship Manager ([Lombard Odier](https://www.lombardodier.com/)). Treating fraud and verification notices as first-class content is an inference from these two sites, not a finding about Nigerian audiences. Still, it is the cheapest trust device on the list, and it fits a market where impersonation of financial firms is a plausible concern (which remains unresearched, see below).

**What could not be seen.** Typefaces, colours, hover states, scroll motion and transitions were not visible for any bank site, because the fetcher returned text, not rendered CSS or JavaScript. Julius Baer, Coutts, Edmond de Rothschild, Bessemer Trust, Patek Philippe and A. Lange & Söhne were not reviewed. A. Lange & Söhne returned HTTP 403. Stonehage Fleming's site now redirects to corient.com, so it no longer exists as a reference ([stonehagefleming.com](https://www.stonehagefleming.com/)). No Nigerian private bank or wealth arm (Stanbic IBTC Wealth, Coronation, FBNQuest Private Wealth) was fetched.

## The 2026 platform makes restrained motion cheap and gimmicks easy

The tooling has shifted in Heirline's favour. **CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: scroll()` / `view()`) became Baseline Newly available in October 2026** when Firefox 159 enabled them, joining Chrome and Edge 115 and Safari 26 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-10-2026)). They can run off the main thread when they animate `transform` and `opacity` ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/scroll-driven-animations)). "Newly available" still leaves many visitors on older browsers, so the right pattern is to make the static, fully composed page the default. Motion is then added inside `@supports (animation-timeline: view())` and `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. **Same-document view transitions are Baseline** (Firefox 144 joined in October 2025), and cross-document transitions via `@view-transition { navigation: auto; }` work in Chrome and Safari, with Firefox partial ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/view-transitions-in-2025); [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions)). So a multi-page site gets quiet page continuity without Barba or Swup. **GSAP, including SplitText and its other former paid plugins, is now 100% free** ([GSAP](https://github.com/greensock/GSAP); [Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free)). That makes choreography cheap, but it also makes the familiar effects ubiquitous.

The usability evidence draws the luxury-versus-gimmick line more sharply than taste does. NN/g's scrolljacking study found such sites "exhibit significant threats to user control and freedom, discoverability, attention, efficiency, and task success". Pages that **altered scroll rate while users had to read text** caused the most severe problems, long scrolljacks made some users think the page was broken, and task-oriented users were far less tolerant than exploratory ones ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). The same study allows a short scrolljack that "gradually adds supporting information to lessen cognitive load". Heirline's visitors are task-oriented (principals, family offices and advisers checking credibility), so the acceptable form is a **`position: sticky` stage over native scroll**: one or two short pinned chapters with a visible end and no change to scroll speed or direction. NN/g's parallax testing, which predates 2025, still found "unimpressed users and a range of usability issues" even in subtler modern forms ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/parallax-usability/)). Layered-hero motion should therefore be close to subliminal: a few percent of offset and a slight overall scale, ending once the hero leaves the viewport. That last specification is inference; no primary source gives offset limits.

For a scrubbed signature moment (say, the 2-of-3 seal assembling), smoothness depends on keyframe density, not JavaScript. Encode **all-intra (`-g 1`) at the rendered size only, `yuv420p`, no audio, faststart**, with VP9 WebM first and H.264 MP4 for Safari. At about 1280px and 30 fps, that produced roughly **8 MB MP4 / 13 MB WebM**, against 1–2 MB for a normal encode, and a 1080p all-intra clip "can blow past 100 MB" ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/scroll-scrubbed-video-stutters-fix)). Those sizes argue for a short, slow, tonal clip, or for a 60–120-frame canvas image sequence when the moment must be frame-exact on iPhone. Lenis 1.3.x remains the standard smooth-scroll layer. It wraps native scroll, keeps `position: sticky` and anchors working, and respects reduced motion by default. It is capped at **60 fps on Safari and 30 fps in Low Power Mode**, and it blocks anchor links unless `anchors: true` is set ([Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)). Thoughtworks flagged accessibility caution ([Thoughtworks Radar](https://thoughtworks.com/radar/languages-and-frameworks/lenis)). For an iPhone-heavy audience, native scroll is the calmer default. Lenis is justified, if at all, on fine-pointer desktops to steady a scrubbed sequence.

The strongest evidence of gimmick status is commoditisation. **Magnetic buttons and custom cursors are sold as drop-in Framer components** whose vendor copy calls them "the hallmark of modern, high-end web design" ([Framer](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/components/magneticbuttons)), and they come as cloneable Webflow templates ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/made-in-webflow/website/Custom-Cursor-Magnetic-Buttons-and-Menu-Interactions)). Apple-style AirPods image-sequence scrubs ship as Webflow cloneables too ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/made-in-webflow/website/gsap-video-animation-on-scroll)). Trend guides describe bento grids as either the new default or already "overused and predictable" ([tinyfrog](https://tinyfrog.com/web-design-trends)), which are opinion pieces. Anything a client can buy for a few dollars cannot signal a bespoke private-client firm. Line-by-line masked reveals of large serif headings are the one kinetic-type pattern still read as editorial. They stay that way only when they fire once per heading, at line level, and never replay. That judgement is the researcher's inference; no design critique was found. The type guidance here is also unverified against any rendered reference site.

## Nigerian HNI trust signals remain an unfilled research gap

The research on Nigerian and diaspora HNI trust signals and on sourced facts usable on the site **did not complete, and there are no notes for it**. This report therefore cannot say which credentials, regulators, professional bodies, land-registry processes, statistics or proof devices Nigerian principals and their UK, US and UAE diaspora relatives find credible, and it does not supply figures to stand in for them. Every Heirline-specific trust device suggested above (process-proof bands, the senior-partner letter, the fraud and verification notice, the private-introduction CTA) is inferred from European private banks, not tested against a Nigerian audience.

Only two Nigeria-related facts appear anywhere in the notes, and neither is a trust signal. Ookla's December 2025 Global Index put **Nigeria's median mobile download speed at 44.14 Mbps, 85th in the world**, as reported in the Nigerian press ([The Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/business-services/nigerias-internet-speed-slips-ranks-85th-globally/)). That matters for performance budgets, not for copy. Estate Intel is summarised in search results as reporting over 1,000 luxury and ultra-luxury units completed in Lagos since 2020 ([Estate Intel](https://estateintel.com/insights/what-is-influencing-the-surge-in-luxury-project-developments-in-lagos)). The underlying article was not read, so the figure should not go on the site without checking the source.

To close the gap, a follow-up round should establish, with primary sources, how land title is verified in Lagos and other key states, the regulators and professional bodies whose names a principal would check (for chartered-accountant sign-off, for instance), the documented fraud and impersonation patterns aimed at Nigerian HNW families, how diaspora heirs research advisers, and which statistics about Nigerian wealth, succession or estate disputes are reliably sourced. Until then, the brand rule against unsourced statistics should be read as a hard stop: the site should carry no market figures at all rather than borrowed ones.

## Accessibility and performance set the real constraints

The non-negotiables come from WCAG Level A and AA, not from taste. **WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A) requires a pause, stop or hide control for any motion that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and runs alongside other content.** That includes a looping ambient hero video. "Starts automatically" also covers motion triggered by scrolling an element into view, and the control must not depend on focus ([W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) asks that non-essential motion caused by interaction, including scroll-linked parallax, can be turned off. Changes to opacity or colour alone do not count as motion, which makes crossfades the safe replacement ([W3C 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). WebKit's taxonomy names five vestibular triggers: scaling and zooming (animated blur included), spinning, multi-speed parallax, 2.5D plane shifts, and peripheral motion near text. It advises removing only known triggers, because over-removal makes a site "unnecessarily boring" ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/7551/responsive-design-for-motion/)). Under reduced motion, then, the hero planes flatten, the scrub becomes a poster or a two-to-three-still crossfade, and line reveals become plain fades. An on-page "Reduce motion" toggle, the W3C's user-set-preference technique, helps older users who do not know the OS setting exists.

Pinned chapters need the whole narrative in the DOM, in reading order, and readable at all times, because screen readers and Tab follow source order rather than scroll position ([DubBot](https://dubbot.com/dubblog/2025/gettin-out-of-a-sticky-situation.html)). Scroll should change emphasis, never presence. Text revealed from `opacity: 0` is still reached by find-in-page and by keyboard focus while invisible. A sticky header must not hide a focused element (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.11, AA) ([W3C 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)). Setting `scroll-padding-top` to the header height handles anchors, Tab and find-in-page together.

Readability for a 50+ audience pushes the type scale up. Health-literacy guidance sets 16px as the floor and suggests **about 19px for older adults** ([HHS](https://odphp.health.gov/healthliteracyonline/design-easy-scanning/use-readable-font-thats-least-16-pixels)). NN/g reports that readability "has remained an issue for seniors throughout all our studies" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-seniors-improvements/)). Contrast floors are 4.5:1 for body and 3:1 for large text at AA, and 7:1 at AAA ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html); [W3C 1.4.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html)). For Heirline, body text should be ink navy on ivory, or ivory on ink navy, aimed at 7:1. Brass suits hairlines, ornaments and large display, and brass body or link text must be measured before use. Two inferences follow, and the brand rules sharpen both. The high-contrast display serif belongs at large sizes only (roughly 32px and up), with the neo-grotesk carrying body and UI. That is typographic convention, not cited legibility research. Text over imagery would normally use a gradient scrim, but the brand bans gradients. The on-brand answer is a solid ink-navy panel, or photography art-directed with a quiet zone where the text sits, checked against the brightest frame.

Performance has to be planned for the tail, not the median. Core Web Vitals "good" at the 75th percentile means **LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms and CLS ≤ 0.1** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). For a video hero, the poster is the LCP candidate. It should be preloaded with `fetchpriority="high"` and never lazy-loaded, and only 17% of mobile pages with an LCP image currently use that hint ([corewebvitals.io](https://corewebvitals.io/core-web-vitals/largest-contentful-paint); [web.dev](https://web.dev/learn/performance/video-performance)). Autoplaying video downloads immediately, even outside the viewport ([web.dev](https://web.dev/learn/performance/video-performance)). **iOS Low Power Mode blocks muted autoplay**, a WebKit bug open since 2020 ([WebKit 216887](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=216887)). **Safari exposes neither `navigator.connection` nor Save-Data** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/adaptive-serving-based-on-network-quality)), so the iPhone majority sends no network signal at all. The practical design is light by default with progressive enhancement. Ship a poster first and catch `video.play()` failures to show a still. Use `100svh` rather than `dvh` for full-screen sections, given documented iOS jank ([WebKit 266835](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=266835)). Use separate portrait crops and clips rather than a centre-cropped 16:9. Leave touch scroll native. The researcher's suggested budgets (about 300 KB to first render, 1 MB or less of initial mobile weight, 3 MB or less per mobile scrub clip) are inferences, sensible starting points to validate under throttled "slow 4G" tests and on a real iPhone in Low Power Mode.

## Patterns to adopt and avoid

| Adopt for Heirline | Why |
|---|---|
| One-sentence hero, one action, an object-led still or slow loop (no people) | Pictet, Hoare and Rothschild grammar. Respects the no-suits rule |
| About five nav items behind a quiet menu, with full-width panels and uppercase group labels | Hoare and Rothschild pattern |
| A process-proof band of dated, verifiable statements (register, title checks, 2-of-3 seal, independent sign-off) in place of AUM or statistics | Copies Pictet's "as at" precision without unsourced numbers |
| Process and chronology as a numbered register or ledger list ruled with hairlines, not cards | Pictet's dated heritage record, Heirline's own metaphor |
| A dated "Letter from the Partner" in place of a blog | Hoare's recurring senior-partner letter |
| One signature pinned chapter (for example the vault seal) on native scroll, short, with a brass hairline progress rule and a still end frame | NN/g: short, purposeful pins keep users oriented |
| Once-only line reveals on display headings and drawn hairlines. Body copy never animated | The editorial pattern that stays luxury when used sparingly |
| CSS scroll-driven animations and cross-document view transitions as progressive enhancement, with the static page as the default | Baseline 2026, off the main thread, calm fallback |
| Native cursor and native scroll. Precise brass underline hovers, gated to `hover: hover` | Avoids commoditised effects and iOS issues |
| Fraud and verification notice plus a "Request a private conversation" close | Rothschild and Lombard Odier precedent |
| Reduce-motion toggle, visible pause on any loop over 5 s, 18–20px body, contrast near 7:1, poster-first lite loading | WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A), 2.3.3, 50+ readability, Nigerian and iPhone conditions |

| Avoid for Heirline | Why |
|---|---|
| Immersive WebGL universes, sound design, 3D product spins | Cartier-style trade-off: accessibility 6.80. Reads as a tech demo |
| Rotating hero carousels and testimonial carousels | Competing messages (Lombard Odier). Testimonials are banned by the brief. Auto-advance needs WCAG 2.2.2 controls |
| Scrolljacking, horizontal pinned galleries, long pins, snapping that fights the user | NN/g: disorientation and task failure |
| Counting-up statistics, AUM boasts, client logo marquees | Unsourced or boastful, and breach discretion |
| Custom or blob cursors, magnetic buttons, cursor trails, spotlight reveals | Sold as marketplace templates. Fail on touch and access |
| Per-letter scrambles, weight "breathing" on scroll, text that scrubs both ways, gradient or outlined marquee type | Read as demo work |
| Bento grids, glassmorphism, mesh or aurora gradients, neon glows, grain everywhere | Startup-template signals. Gradients and glass are banned by the brand |
| Gradient scrims behind text | Brand conflict. Use solid ink panels or art-directed quiet zones |
| Preloader percentage counters, full-screen wipes on every click | Delay content for no meaning |
| Opacity-0 reveals on real text, Lenis `syncTouch` on iOS, `100dvh` heroes, lazy-loaded hero poster | Accessibility and performance failures documented above |
| Any Nigerian market statistic not yet traced to a primary source | Topic 3 research is incomplete |

## Conclusion

The research reframes the brief's restraint as a competitive position rather than a constraint to work around. No private bank, trust company or notary has won an Awwwards Site of the Day in 2023–2026 in the archive searched, the Nigerian category appears to be template-grade, and the 2026 platform lets a small team ship native, compositor-friendly motion with a graceful static fallback. A site that is visibly crafted yet reads perfectly with motion switched off would therefore stand apart. The decisive design problem is no longer which effect to use. It is how to make *process* (registers, titles, seals, sign-off) as legible and dated as Pictet makes two centuries of history, without a single borrowed number.

Two pieces of work remain before motion and content specs are final. A designer should open Pictet, Hoare, Rothschild, Cartier W&W, Kümmerlein, Yamauchi No.10 and Haute Capital in a real browser, because every typographic and motion observation here was inferred from text. And the Nigerian and diaspora trust-signal research needs to be run from scratch. It will decide which credentials, verifications and notices appear in the proof band and footer, and until it is done that content should stay empty rather than invented.
